# Kenya Informatics Olympiad (KIO)
## Structure, Eligibility, and Selection Calendar 2026–2027

### 1. Overview
The Kenya Informatics Olympiad (KIO) is the premier national competition identifying and developing algorithmic problem-solving and programming talent among secondary school students in Kenya. The KIO serves as the official selection pathway for the national team that will represent Kenya at the Pan-African Informatics Olympiad (PAIO) and the International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI).

### 2. Eligibility Requirements
To ensure compliance with international standards (such as IOI regulations) and to promote fair competition, participants must meet the following criteria:
*   **Educational Status**: Must be officially enrolled in a recognized secondary school in Kenya (typically Form 1 to Form 4, or equivalent Grade 9 to Grade 12).
*   **Age Limit**: Must be under 20 years of age as of July 1st of the year of the international competition (e.g., July 1, 2027, for the 2027 IOI). 
*   **Residency/Citizenship**: Must be a Kenyan citizen or a student who has been studying in Kenya for at least two consecutive years.
*   **University Enrollment**: Students who have already commenced university or higher education studies are ineligible to participate.

### 3. Official Three-Round Structure
The KIO is structured in three progressive rounds. Rounds 1 and 2 are designed to be flexible depending on available funding, while Round 3 requires strict in-person attendance to ensure the integrity of the final team selection.

| Round | Format & Scoring | Mode of Delivery | Duration | Content Style | Selection Goal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Round 1 (Screening)** | 20 Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQ).<br>Section A: 12 qs × 1 mark.<br>Section B: 8 qs × 2 marks.<br>*Total: 28 marks.* | **Online preferred** (via edureach.site or equivalent).<br>*In-person regional hubs only if funded.* | 2.5 hours | Pure computational thinking, discrete math, graph theory, combinatorics, and geometry puzzles. *No coding required.* | Broad national reach; identify students with raw logical and algorithmic aptitude. Automated marking. |
| **Round 2 (Semi-final)** | 4 Questions × 25 marks.<br>*Total: 100 marks.*<br>Per question:<br>- Algorithm/Pseudocode (10m)<br>- Code implementation (15m) | **In-person preferred** (at CEMASTEA).<br>*Hybrid/Online fallback if funding is insufficient.* | 3 hours | Mixed written and implementation tasks (e.g., sequences, computational geometry, optimization, greedy algorithms in C/C++/Python). | Test both theoretical algorithmic design and practical coding ability. |
| **Round 3 (Final / Team Selection)** | Standard IOI/Codeforces-style competitive programming problems. | **Mandatory In-person**. | 1 or 2 days<br>(5 hrs each) | Pure competitive programming on a secure platform (e.g., Codeforces Gym). IOI and PAIO level difficulty. | Final selection of the National Team (4 contestants + alternates). |

### 4. Shortlist Sizes & Progression Guidelines
The progression from Round 1 to the final team is highly competitive. Cut-offs may be adjusted slightly based on the difficulty of the papers each year.

*   **After Round 1**: Target of **80–120 students** advancing. Generally, the top 15–20% of participants, or those scoring $\ge 16/28$. Efforts will be made to ensure geographic and gender diversity.
*   **After Round 2**: Target of **24–36 students** advancing to the finals. Generally, the top 25–30% of Round 2 participants, or those scoring $\ge 45/100$.
*   **After Round 3**: The top **4 contestants** are selected as the official National Team. The next 2–4 ranking students are selected as alternates. Alternates train with the main team and can step in if a primary member is unable to attend international events.
